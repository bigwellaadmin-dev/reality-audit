# Reality Audit Rubric (A–E)

*Aligned to QCAA standards-elaboration format. C represents the achievement standard.*

This rubric assesses the Reality Audit **process** — how the student worked with the AI — not the quality of the final product. It is designed to be used alongside subject-specific rubrics that assess content knowledge and skill, not instead of them. Grade the loop, not the polish.

**Source:** Sandra Robinson, *Breaking the Loop: The Reality Audit Framework, Part 4: Assessment and Accountability*. Licensed CC BY 4.0.

| Criterion | A — Extending | B — Above standard | C — Achievement standard | D — Approaching standard | E — Below standard |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Intention** — Predictive Calibration | Calibrates state precisely and uses it to set the AI's role deliberately (challenger, connector, or teacher), explaining why that role fits the task and the moment. | Declares a RAG state and chooses an AI role that matches it. Can explain the reasoning behind the choice. | Declares a knowledge/emotional state before opening the AI and chooses a broadly appropriate way to use it. | Opens the AI with little or no calibration. May declare a state but does not let it shape how the AI is used. | No evidence of calibration. Goes straight to the tool, often from a vulnerable state, asking for a finished product. |
| **Interaction** — The Friction Protocol | Systematically forces friction, using anti-sycophancy stems to make the AI argue, then wrestles with the pushback. The Interaction Log is rich with genuine challenge. | Uses Friction Stems to make the AI disagree on at least the central claim and engages with the result. | Asks the AI to find at least one flaw or counter-argument rather than only requesting content. Some friction is visible in the log. | Asks mostly for content or confirmation. One weak attempt at challenge, or none that is acted on. | The log is a series of requests and acceptances. The AI was never made to push back. |
| **Response** — The Fluency Audit | Consistently audits own reaction, identifies Relief as a warning, and acts on it by re-examining the comfortable claims. Distinguishes fluency from soundness throughout. | Identifies at least one Relief moment and returns to check the claim behind it. Treats comfort as a signal. | Notices and names a reaction (Relief or Curiosity) to the AI output and recognises that fluency is not accuracy. | Accepts the AI's framing where it agrees most easily. Little evidence of auditing own reaction. | Absorbs the AI's confident wording wholesale. No point of resistance anywhere in the work. |
| **Reflection** — Social Triangulation | Takes the argument to a genuinely disagreeing human and to an independent source, and visibly revises the work in response to the friction that produces. | Runs the central claim past a Difficult Human or an outside source and integrates what comes back. | Seeks at least one perspective from outside the chat — a human or a source — relevant to the task. | A superficial outside reference that largely repeats the AI, or a human check that did not actually disagree. | No outside voice. Every claim is downstream of the chat window. |
| **Academic Branch** — Prove You Learned It | Reproduces the reasoning cold on a near-transfer problem, triangulates the key claim against an independent source, and documents the Chain of Learning. | Completes a near-transfer task with minor support and confirms a key claim in a second source. | Can explain the core reasoning without the tool open and points to one confirming source. | Can summarise the output but struggles to reproduce the reasoning cold. Thin or absent log. | Cannot reconstruct the reasoning without the tool. No log, no external confirmation — borrowed understanding. |
