# Part 4: Assessment and Accountability

*Sandra Robinson, Big Wella · Breaking the Loop · The Reality Audit Framework · Teacher Guide*

Reality Audit changes what you assess. The polished output is the least informative thing a student produces in an AI-enabled task, because an agreeable machine can generate a polished output for anyone. What carries information is the *process* — the calibration, the friction, the reaction, the triangulation, and whether the learning survives the tool being closed. This part is about assessing that.

## 4.1 The core shift: grade the loop, not the polish

The traditional model assesses the product. In an AI-enabled classroom that model breaks, because the product no longer reliably indicates the learning. Reality Audit's answer is not to detect and punish AI use. It is to make the process visible and assessable, and to move a meaningful share of the grade onto it.

> **The pedagogical shift in one line**
>
> Move 50% of the grade to the Reality Audit Log — the record of how the student calibrated their intent, challenged the AI, audited their reaction, and verified the response. Grade the struggle, not the polish.

Two task types make this concrete:

- **The Reality Audit Log.** Students submit the record of their interaction — RAG state, the Friction Stems they used, the R/C annotations, the Difficult Human's response, the Triangulation source — alongside the product. Reward the logs that show argument and revision. A log of "write me X / thanks, perfect" scores low however good the output looks.
- **The Debunking Task.** Give students an overly agreeable, subtly wrong AI response and have them find the three logical errors hidden behind the kind language. This assesses the Fluency Audit directly, and it cannot be outsourced to the tool that produced the error.

## 4.2 Task design for an AI-enabled classroom

You cannot make a task "AI-proof," and trying to is the wrong goal. You can make a task *AI-revealing* — one where thoughtful AI use leaves a visible trace and frictionless shortcutting leaves a visible absence.

- **Require the Interaction Log.** If the process is part of the submission, the student who skipped it pays a cost you can see.
- **Build in a Near-Transfer component.** A short, in-class, tool-closed task on a related problem. It is the cheapest, strongest check on whether the learning transferred.
- **Ask for the reasoning, not the conclusion.** "Argue the opposite of your thesis" or "justify each decision without the AI" cannot be answered from a borrowed understanding.
- **Anchor to genuine stakes.** A task the student actually has a position on produces real friction and real reflection. A generic task produces a generic loop.
- **Assess process without surveillance.** The goal is growth, not policing. Use portfolios that show development over time. Make logs formative, not punitive — a student who documents an honest struggle should never score worse than one who hid a frictionless shortcut.

## 4.3 The two rubrics

Two rubric formats are provided as standalone files for direct classroom use. Both assess the Reality Audit *process* and are designed to sit alongside your subject's content rubric, not replace it.

- **[The A–E rubric](../rubrics/reality-audit-rubric-a-to-e.md)** — aligned to the QCAA standards-elaboration format, with C as the achievement standard. Use it where your reporting context expects A–E.
- **[The four-level rubric](../rubrics/reality-audit-rubric-four-level.md)** — Emerging / Developing / Proficient / Advanced. Use it for formative tracking and portfolio assessment.

The shape of the four-level rubric, in brief:

| Level | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Emerging** | Uses AI without reflection. Accepts output without challenge. Feels Relief and stops. No Friction Stems, no log. |
| **Developing** | Can identify intention and reaction. Declares a RAG state and names Relief vs Curiosity, but does not yet act on them. |
| **Proficient** | Runs the loop. Calibrates, uses Friction Stems, notices and acts on Relief, triangulates against at least one outside source. |
| **Advanced** | Runs the loop and proves it. Checks claims against real sources, completes a Near-Transfer task without AI, documents the Chain of Learning, and analyses the systemic context. |

## 4.4 Feedback language for every stage

Frame feedback around the stage to revisit and a concrete next move — not general praise or criticism. Use these as starting points; adapt to your student and, especially, to their access (do not send "find an authoritative source" to a student working mobile-only — frame Triangulation around a class text or a human they can reach).

**Intention**
- *Strong:* You named your state before you started and chose the right move for it — you used the AI as a challenger because you already knew the material. That is the calibration the loop asks for.
- *Developing:* You opened the tool without checking your state. Next task, write Red/Amber/Green first, and let it decide whether you ask the AI to teach you or to argue with you.
- *Weak:* There is no sign you asked why you were using AI for this. The work reads as if you came for a finished answer, not for thinking.

**Interaction**
- *Strong:* Your Interaction Log shows you forcing the AI to find flaws in your own argument and then wrestling with what it said. That friction is the work.
- *Developing:* You asked clear questions but never asked the AI to disagree with you. Next task, use a Friction Stem — make it argue the other side before you accept anything.
- *Weak:* The log is a series of requests and acceptances. The AI was never made to push back, so nothing in this work was tested.

**Response**
- *Strong:* You flagged a moment where the answer felt too comfortable and went back to check it. Noticing the Relief and acting on it is the skill.
- *Developing:* You accepted the AI's framing where it agreed with you most easily. Next task, when a part feels like Relief, treat that as the signal to slow down, not speed up.
- *Weak:* The work absorbs the AI's confident wording without a single point of resistance. There is no evidence you audited your reaction to it.

**Reflection**
- *Strong:* You took the AI's claim to a real person who disagreed, and the work is sharper for the argument that followed.
- *Developing:* Everything here is downstream of the chat. Next task, run your main claim past one Difficult Human and bring back what they said.
- *Weak:* There is no outside voice anywhere in this. The only thing that agreed with this argument is the machine built to agree with you.

**Academic Branch**
- *Strong:* You reproduced the core of this on a blank page and confirmed the key claim in a second source. The understanding is yours.
- *Developing:* The output is polished but you couldn't explain it cold when I asked. That gap is Cognitive Debt — the learning hasn't transferred yet.
- *Weak:* There is no Interaction Log, no second source, and you couldn't reconstruct the reasoning without the tool. This is borrowed understanding, not earned.

## 4.5 A note on integrity

Reality Audit is not an academic-integrity instrument and should not be sold to a staff or a community as one. Its purpose is to keep a student's thinking alive in contact with an agreeable machine — not to catch cheats. In assessment, treat the absence of friction as *formative information about where to teach next*, not as an accusation.

There is a real integrity question Reality Audit does not solve: the student who knows the work is not theirs and is fine with that. What Reality Audit does is make genuine thinking visible enough that its absence is also visible. The response to a confirmed integrity breach is a matter for your school's policy and your professional judgement. Reality Audit's contribution is to make the breach legible in the first place — and, far more often, to make the breach unnecessary by teaching the judgement that prevents it.

> **The measure of success**
>
> The true measure is not how cleanly you integrate AI or how reliably you detect it. It is whether your students leave able to use a powerful, agreeable tool without being quietly flown by it. That is what these rubrics are trying to see, and it is the only outcome that matters.
