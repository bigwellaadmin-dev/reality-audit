# Reality Audit Rubric (Four-Level)

*Emerging · Developing · Proficient · Advanced. For formative tracking and portfolio assessment.*

This rubric assesses the Reality Audit **process**, not the final product. Use it to track a student's development across multiple tasks — the pattern over time matters more than any single result. Grade the loop, not the polish.

**Source:** Sandra Robinson, *Breaking the Loop: The Reality Audit Framework, Part 4: Assessment and Accountability*. Licensed CC BY 4.0.

| Stage | Emerging | Developing | Proficient | Advanced |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Intention** | Opens the AI with no calibration, often from a vulnerable state. | Declares a RAG state but does not let it shape how the AI is used. | Calibrates and matches the AI's role (teacher / connector / challenger) to the state. | Calibrates precisely and explains why the chosen role fits this task and this moment. |
| **Interaction** | Requests and accepts; no friction. | One attempt to challenge the AI, not acted on. | Uses Friction Stems on the central claim and engages with the pushback. | Forces friction systematically; the log is rich with genuine, acted-on challenge. |
| **Response** | Absorbs the AI's framing; feels Relief and stops. | Names a reaction but does not act on it. | Identifies Relief as a warning and re-checks the comfortable claims. | Audits reaction throughout; reliably separates fluency from soundness. |
| **Reflection** | No voice but the machine's. | A token outside reference that did not disagree. | Seeks a genuinely disagreeing human or source and integrates it. | Triangulates against both a Difficult Human and an independent source; revises visibly. |
| **Academic Branch** | Cannot reconstruct the reasoning without the tool; no log. | Summarises the output but struggles cold. | Explains the core reasoning tool-closed and cites one confirming source. | Passes a near-transfer task, triangulates the key claim, documents the Chain of Learning. |

## How to read the overall pattern

A single stage at Emerging is not a problem to fix in isolation — use the **Academic Branch diagnostic** (Part 2) to find where the loop actually broke down. A student who is Advanced on Interaction but Emerging on the Academic Branch has a different teaching need from one who never calibrates. Read the row that fails *backwards* to the stage that caused it.

- **Mostly Emerging across the loop** → the student is in the Hype-Man loop. Start with Intention and Response only; build the two fastest habits before adding friction.
- **Strong early, weak at the Branch** → Cognitive Debt. The loop ran but the learning did not transfer. Add Near-Transfer checks to the next tasks.
- **Strong everywhere except Reflection** → a closed circuit with the machine. Make the Difficult Human mandatory and named in advance.
